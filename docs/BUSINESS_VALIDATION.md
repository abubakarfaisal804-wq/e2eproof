# Business validation — evidence before scaling

The software existing does not prove that a profitable business exists.

## Narrow initial customer

Independent developers and small teams shipping AI-built web apps that already have at least one important flow involving a form, payment, webhook, account action, or external AI provider.

## Initial promise

> “Prove that your three most important user flows caused the real backend outcome — and catch fake success, duplicate actions and hidden fallbacks before users do.”

## First self-service offer

- Free local CLI: one repository, manual runs.
- Paid later: hosted scheduled runs, deployment triggers, history, alerts and team evidence.
- Price hypotheses to test: €19 / €49 / €99 per month.

## Proof gates before building hosted SaaS

1. At least 20 external repositories run the CLI.
2. At least 5 users report a real defect found.
3. At least 5 users agree to pay for automated hosted runs.
4. At least 3 complete a paid pilot or pre-order with written scope and refund terms.

## Distribution experiments

- Publish the open-source core on GitHub.
- Create short technical case studies: fake success, duplicate charge, silent fallback.
- Post useful examples in relevant developer communities without mass-spam.
- Ship a GitHub Action so use starts in an existing workflow.
- Track installs, completed runs, retained weekly projects, defects found and paid intent.

## Stop/pivot conditions

Pause hosted development when, after meaningful distribution:

- fewer than 10% of installers complete a first contract;
- users cannot define independent read-backs;
- existing tools solve the same problem sufficiently;
- support effort destroys self-service economics;
- paid intent remains absent despite repeated real defect detection.

## Revenue reality

At €49/month, roughly 103 active customers produce €5,047 monthly recurring revenue before taxes, payment fees, hosting, support and refunds. That is a target model, not a forecast or guarantee.
